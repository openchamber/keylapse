# Keylapse design

## Window

Settings look like a macOS desktop widget: a frosted-glass window (`NSVisualEffectView`, HUD material, behind-window blending) with the desktop blurred through it, dimmed by a black layer at 30% so text stays readable over bright wallpapers. The content sits straight on the glass in translucent groups. The title bar is transparent and the window can be dragged from anywhere. It opens where it was last left, centred only the first time, and always on the display it was asked for from: opened from the menu bar of another display, it takes the same place on that one; when its height changes it keeps its top edge. The window title is hidden; the app name appears once, in the page header.

The window is 520 pt wide and as tall as its content, never scrolling as a whole: it grows and shrinks with the number of layouts and the setup state. At the top, the flower glyph and the app name. Section headings are 11 pt bold, uppercase, with 0.8 pt tracking in secondary colour. Rows are 13 pt medium; supporting text 11–12 pt medium in secondary colour. Use 20 pt side margins, 20 pt between sections and 8 pt between a heading and its group.

## Sections

Each section is a heading over one translucent group: white at 10%, continuous 12 pt corners, rows 38 pt tall with 12 pt side padding and 1 pt hairlines (white at 12%) between them, inset from the left. Sections share the frame but not the control, so the page does not read as a stack of identical pills:

- **Header** is two columns like a calendar widget: on the left the 42 pt pixel-art flower glyph beside Keylapse at 34 pt light on one line, in a 220 pt column, with Setup and Behavior beneath once setup is complete; on the right the Layouts section, its heading level with Setup. The glyph is the same pixel flower as the menu bar. The title bar shows no text.
- **Layouts** has no group: each layout is a row with the language at 22 pt light and the variant beneath in 12 pt secondary, separated by hairlines. The column starts level with the Setup heading and ends with the last Behavior row: three rows share the measured height of that block (about 60 pt each; below 54 pt the type steps down to 19/11 pt), so the two columns read as one block under the title. Up to three rows show outright; with more, the list becomes a loop: after the last layout comes the first again, both edges fade out over 24 pt, and the active layout is always scrolled to the middle, on launch and whenever it changes (Fn, a click on a row or a tick). Instead of a scrollbar a tick rail sits at the right edge: one 2 pt capsule per layout at a 12 pt pitch, white at 30%, the active layout's tick 14 pt long and solid, others 10 pt. Hovering stretches the tick under the pointer to 20 pt with the stretch falling off over three neighbours (1, 0.6, 0.35, 0.15); clicking a tick makes that layout active and scrolls to it. The Layouts column is always inset a further 30 pt on the right; the rail sits in that inset, so nothing moves when it appears. The left column keeps the page margin. The whole column is right-aligned: heading, names and variants end at the window margin; the active layout is full white with a 7 pt accent dot just before its name, and the others sit at 70%. Layouts without correction carry a secondary Switching only tag beneath the name.
- **Behavior** (Switch on key release, Launch at login, Correct on menu bar click) sits in the left column under Setup in the same quiet style: a heading and 12 pt regular rows with a mini system switch on the left, aligned with the Setup checkmarks, no group. An enabled switch shows the accent colour. Labels are white whenever the option applies; a row that does not apply right now (Switch on key release while Fn alone is not the switch shortcut) stays in place with its switch disabled and the label secondary, showing what actually happens.
- **Setup** while incomplete is a full-width group above Layouts: the step name on the left and a small system button on the right (Grant…, Open Settings). Under the group one secondary line, Your text stays on your Mac. Nothing is sent., because this is the moment the user is asked to trust the app; it goes away with the group. The Fn key action step is always listed, dimmed to 45% while no shortcut has Fn in it, so the group and the status list never change height. Once every step is ready it leaves the groups and becomes a quiet status list under the app name: a SETUP heading and one 12 pt regular line per step with a plain bold checkmark, all in secondary colour. It reads as finished work, so it carries no accent.
- **Shortcuts** holds, per row, the action with a two-line hint on the left and the key illustration, which is also the control, at the right edge. Hovering the keys brightens them with a thin white edge and shows the pointing-hand cursor. Rows are a fixed 66 pt and the hint always has room for two lines, so the window never jumps while recording. A shortcut that cannot work yet does not look ready: while macOS still keeps the Fn key for itself, every row whose shortcut has Fn in it sits at 50% (name and keycaps) and its hint reads Not working yet: set Fn to “Do Nothing” above, or click the keys to use others.

## Welcome page

The first time the window opens, and until Start using Keylapse is clicked, the same glass shows a welcome page instead of the settings. Show, don't tell: no line on it is longer than eight words. At the top, the same logo as on the settings page and in the same place, so the header does not move when Start swaps the pages: the 42 pt pixel flower beside Keylapse at 34 pt light, left-aligned, and under it one line at 20 pt regular in secondary colour, large enough to be read before anything else on the page: Fixes text typed in the wrong layout. Then a Setup group with the three steps as in the settings, each with a reason beneath in 11 pt secondary (To replace the text you select. / To see the shortcut keys. / So Fn can switch layouts.) and the Grant…, Open Settings or checkmark at the right. Input Monitoring nearly always arrives with Accessibility, and the page says so instead of promising a second grant: until Accessibility is granted its row has no button and reads Usually comes with Accessibility.; when it did arrive that way the checkmark comes with Came with Accessibility.; only if it is still missing after Accessibility does the row get its Grant… and To see the shortcut keys.; rows are 8 pt taller than settings rows to fit the reason. The third step is about Fn. While either shortcut has Fn in it and macOS has not been told to leave Fn alone, its name says what the key is for, as in the finished row (Switch layouts with Fn, or Correct text with Control + Fn when only the correction has Fn), the line beneath says what to do about it (Set Fn to “Do Nothing” in macOS.), and at the right are Open Settings and, to its left, a second small button, Use other keys: two buttons for two options, since nobody should have to hand Fn over to get through setup. Open Settings is the prominent one while the step is current; Use other keys is a quiet button, never a text link. Clicking it starts recording right in the row, and the row says so in its largest text: the title becomes Press a key to switch layouts, breathing on the beat, the line beneath says Esc cancels. (or what is held so far, or why a key was refused, in red), and the row's keycaps take the accent edge with a breathing accent glow. Wherever keys are being recorded they glow like this, so waiting for a key press always looks like waiting. Doing without Fn means both shortcuts without it, so a recording started by Use other keys refuses keys with Fn in them (macOS keeps Fn for itself. Press keys without Fn., in red, and keeps recording): once the switch shortcut is recorded and the correction still has Fn, the same row goes straight on to Now press keys to correct text with the correction keycaps; if only the correction has Fn, Use other keys starts there. The disabled Try it block below shows none of this. Once the step is done, whether Fn was given to Keylapse in macOS or another shortcut was recorded, the row looks the same: Switch layouts, Click the keys to change them., the keycaps that switch (Fn or the recorded ones, still clickable to record others) and the checkmark. So the switch shortcut can be changed at any point of the welcome page, not only before the step is completed. Until Accessibility and Input Monitoring are there the whole row is disabled, buttons and keycaps alike, like Try it: a key chosen then could not switch anything and would only look broken. If the permissions are taken away after a key was recorded, the row has no checkmark, sits at 50% and reads Works once Accessibility is granted. Under the group: Your text stays on your Mac. Nothing is sent.

Then a Try it group of two rows. The upper one (50 pt) is a plain 17 pt text field holding hello as it comes out typed on the other layout (руддщ on Ukrainian, read from the real macOS tables, so it needs English plus one more supported layout; otherwise the group is absent; the word is taken from the first layout in the list whose letters alone tell which layout typed it and never changes when the active layout changes), with an accent checkmark at its right once the word reads hello. The lower one (58 pt) is built like the Switch layouts row: the action by the name it has in Shortcuts and in the menu bar menu, Correct selected text, the instruction beneath in 11 pt, and at the right a quiet button and the correction keycaps, the same recordable control as in Shortcuts. The instruction reads Press these keys, or set your own. and the button Set my own (it starts the recording and reads Cancel while it runs, when the instruction is Press a key or combination. or what is held or why a key was refused, in red). Once the setup steps are done the word is selected, ready for the shortcut, and selected again after a recording; the page never asks the user to select it, and if a click has taken the selection away the shortcut takes the whole word again. While Try it is the next step its row follows the rule of every next step: the name steps back to 70%, the instruction is white and breathes, the keycaps have a breathing accent edge. The correction is triggered by the real shortcut and uses the real layout tables; in Keylapse's own field the word is replaced directly through the text system (undoable with ⌘Z) instead of going through the clipboard. Try it is as law-abiding as a correction anywhere else: the same rules about the active layout apply and the same Correct to chooser appears with three or more correctable layouts. When the real correction would refuse, the instruction says so instead: Switch to Ukrainian first, then press the keys., or, while a Russian layout is active and the word could have been typed on it, Switch away from Russian, then press the keys. When the field reads hello the keycaps stay, no longer breathing, the instruction says in white Fixed. These keys work in every app., and the button becomes Again, which puts the wrong word back; that ⌘Z undoes a correction is in its help. The shortcut is the one thing the user must take away from this page, so it never leaves the screen. While setup is incomplete the group sits at 45% with Finish the setup above first. and nothing in it responds: the keys and Set my own are disabled until the last setup step is done.

The page leads. At any moment exactly one thing is the next step, in this order: Accessibility, Input Monitoring, the Fn key action, Try it, Start. Its button is the only prominent (accent) control in the window and its accent glow breathes on a 1.3 s beat; for Try it, the keycaps get a breathing accent edge instead. The reason under the current setup step turns white and breathes with the button, down to 50% on the dim half of the beat, and the step's name above it steps back to 70% so the breathing line is the brightest thing in the row. Never accent-coloured text on the glass: it is too dark to read there. Steps whose turn has not come sit at 50%; the switch-key row and Try it do not respond until the permissions are granted; done steps are full strength with their checkmark. The same rule applies to the Setup group on the settings page.

At the bottom, a Launch at login mini switch on the left (disabled at 50% while a setup step is left, like everything else whose turn has not come) and the Start using Keylapse button on the right, disabled (lettering at 45%) while a setup step is left, since there is nothing to start using yet, then quiet while Try it is the next step so the demo can be skipped, and prominent once it is the next step itself, then a line with the 17 pt flower: Click the menu bar flower to come back. Start shows the settings page and is remembered; closing the window once every setup step is done counts the same, so a user who set everything up and closed the page is not welcomed again; Show Welcome Page in the menu bar menu brings the page back, touching nothing else. The window keeps its width and grows or shrinks with the page.

## Buttons

A button answers the click that also brings the window forward; nothing has to be clicked twice after System Settings or the chooser was in front. Two kinds only. The next step is the system prominent button in the accent colour, breathing. Every other button (Use other keys, Open Settings and Grant… before their turn, Set my own, Cancel, Again, Reset, Start using Keylapse before its turn) is quiet: white lettering in 11 pt medium on a white 20% fill with 6 pt corners, 20 pt tall (13 pt, 24 pt tall and 7 pt corners for Start), brighter while pressed, lettering at 45% when disabled. It looks the same whether the window is active or not. Do not use the system bordered style: in an active window it writes the title in the accent colour, which cannot be read on the glass.

## Colour

Always use a dark appearance.

| Role | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Text | white |
| Secondary text | white at 62% |
| Group surface | white at 10% |
| Hairline | white at 12% |
| Accent | the macOS accent colour from System Settings |

The system accent colour marks checkmarks, enabled switches, menus and the Active tag, so the window matches the rest of the Mac. The flower itself is always the same one-colour pixel-art glyph everywhere: the silhouette outline one cell thick with a pixel dot in the middle. In the window it is `FlowerGlyph.png` (84 px, 21-cell grid) drawn at 42 pt without interpolation so cells land on whole pixels, and `FlowerGlyphSmall.png` (34 px, 17-cell grid) at 17 pt in the chooser; in the menu bar the 21-cell template images at 21 pt; on the app icon the 21-cell grid over the blurred photo. Avoid outlines, shadows around sections, and any solid opaque surface behind the content.

## Settings rows

Setup combines three steps: Accessibility, Input Monitoring, and Fn key. The Fn step applies while either shortcut has Fn in it, alone or with other keys: macOS does not let any of them through while it keeps Fn for an action of its own. When the system Fn action is not Do Nothing, show one row: Set [monochrome globe glyph] Fn to “Do Nothing” on the left and a small Open Settings button on the right. No keycap illustration, arrow, caption, or vertical explanation in this row. Keep the macOS instruction in help and accessibility text. Once correct, replace the row with a static accent checkmark and Fn key. Do not show a separate Fn banner or a settings button in Layouts. Prefer AppleFnUsageType over the legacy AppleFnUsage; if neither can be read, present the action as a setting to check without claiming a conflict. Refresh on launch, activation, and normal state refresh; never change the system setting automatically.

Group related rows under Layouts, Behavior, Setup and Shortcuts. Allow taller rows for wrapped names. Enabled controls use the system accent. Preserve native checkbox behaviour.

When ready, order the sections as Layouts, Behavior alongside Setup, then Shortcuts. The Layouts list mirrors enabled, selectable macOS keyboard input sources in system enumeration order. Show a small order number and a precise, left-aligned name that distinguishes variants of the same language. Clicking a layout, or its tick on the rail, makes it the active input source and scrolls it into view; otherwise the list is informational, without dropdowns, exclusions, buttons, or add/remove controls. Refresh on system changes without resetting other preferences. Shortcuts belong last, as two rows with key illustrations that record a replacement when clicked. Behavior checkbox labels fill the row so the entire label area responds to clicks.

Fn cycles through all sources, including input methods. Correction requires a keyboard-layout table with enough letters; composition input methods and excluded languages are not eligible. Label unsupported sources Switching only, with explanatory help. Require at least two supported layouts for correction, and give a brief explanation otherwise. The layout the selected text was typed on is worked out before anything is shown or changed, and never guessed. First the obvious: every correctable layout that cannot type one of the text's letters is ruled out (only the plain and Shift keys count; a letter none of them types rules every layout out, so text is refused rather than left half converted). One layout left is the source, whatever is active. Several left: the active layout is the source if it is one of them; if not, the error Switch to the layout used to type this text, then try again. appears at once. None left: when the letters come from two of the user's layouts together, Select only the mistyped text.; when one of them is a letter none of the correctable layouts types (ъ), Keylapse can’t correct some of these letters., followed by Крим це Україна. when a Russian layout is enabled and the letter comes from it (the owner's own line, in Ukrainian on purpose). Russian takes part in this only while it is the active layout: text whose every letter a Russian layout types is then taken as typed on it and refused (Keylapse doesn’t correct Russian. Крим це Україна.); text it cannot have typed (Greek, Latin, Cyrillic with і, ї, є or ґ) is corrected by the ordinary rule whatever is active. Only then, with more than one possible destination, the chooser lists the other correctable layouts, and any choice there succeeds: an error never follows the chooser. With one possible destination the text is corrected straight away. After a correction the destination layout becomes the active one. Ignore legacy app-specific selection preferences.

While any setup step is incomplete, show Setup as a group at the top, retaining its small heading. Read actual macOS state. Once all steps are ready, move it under the app name as a status list and show Behavior beneath it and Shortcuts full width at the bottom.

Keep rows aligned and preserve keyboard navigation. Manage layout additions and removals through macOS Keyboard Settings.

Each missing permission has a small Grant… button on the right opening the corresponding macOS settings. After the click, while macOS has not granted it yet, the button gives way to a replica of the row to find in System Settings: the app icon, Keylapse, and a mini switch that flips on and off every 1.3 s, on a white 10% pill with 7 pt corners. It shows the gesture instead of describing it; clicking it opens the pane again. On the welcome page the reason under the step changes to the one thing left to do (Turn on Keylapse in the list. / Not listed? Click + and add Keylapse.). The row keeps its height. While waiting, the same replica at 1.5× floats over the bottom edge of the System Settings window, 28 pt in from its right side: glass darkened by black at 55% with a 2 pt accent border and 14 pt corners, half of it over the window so it cannot be taken for part of the desktop, with a 2 pt accent line rising 24 pt from it into the window and ending in a 9 pt accent dot ringed in white. On the Fn step, where Keylapse already has Accessibility and can read where things are in System Settings, the tag hangs from the Press 🌐 key to menu itself: the dot sits on the menu's lower edge and follows it as the pane scrolls; while that menu is open the tag is hidden, so it never covers the items to choose from, and it returns if the menu closes unchanged; the tag appears only once the menu has been found, so it never shows at the edge first and jumps; if the menu cannot be found within 2.5 s or is scrolled out of the window, the tag sits at the bottom edge. On the Accessibility step it cannot know where the Keylapse row is, so it stays at the bottom edge. It breathes with the switch: the accent glow around it swells and it grows by 4%. The glow fades out completely inside the tag's own panel (40 pt of clear room on the sides and below), so its edge is never visible as a rectangle. Keylapse is always dark, so on a light System Settings the tag is the darkest thing there; it follows the window, hides when System Settings is not on screen, never takes focus, and disappears with the replica the moment the permission arrives, when the Keylapse window closes or when Start is clicked. When System Settings opens over the Keylapse window, Keylapse moves its own window to the side of it with more room (16 pt apart, kept on that screen, animated, once per wait), so the row with the flipping switch stays in view beside the real one. When System Settings goes away, finished or just closed, the window slides back to where it stood, unless the user has dragged it in the meantime. The macOS Accessibility dialog appears on the main display wherever the Keylapse window is; if that is another display, the window goes over beside the dialog, and returns to its place with the rest. When macOS opens System Settings on another display, the two are brought together the same once: on the Fn step, where Keylapse has Accessibility, it moves System Settings beside its own window; on the Accessibility step it cannot move another app's window, so it goes to that display itself. The Fn step waits the same way after Open Settings: its replica is the Keyboard settings row, Press 🌐 key to with a Do Nothing menu value that lights up in accent and fades back, and the reason becomes Choose this in System Settings. The line points at the replica beside it instead of naming Do Nothing a second time. While waiting, the real state is re-read every 0.6 s, so the checkmark lands at once. When a step completes, System Settings quits if it was not open before the click, and the Keylapse window comes forward. When System Settings is closed without the step being done, the wait ends: the replica gives way to the step's buttons again, so the other way through (Use other keys) is never locked out. Once granted, replace the button with a non-interactive accent checkmark. Reflect actual system access; if revoked, show the grant button again. Do not use toggles or editable checkboxes for permissions.

Dynamic text (hints, notices) lives in space reserved for it; nothing may be truncated and no section may change height while the user interacts with it. Hide the entire Behavior section while any setup step is incomplete. Reveal it automatically once all three are ready, preserving all stored preference values. If setup becomes incomplete again, hide Behavior without resetting preferences.

## App icon

A blurred square of the dusk-mountain wallpaper (slate blue and copper) inside the standard rounded square, darkened by 18%, with the flower as white pixel art: the five-circle silhouette sampled on a 21 × 21 grid of 30 px cells (630 px, about 75% of the square, so the flower has air around it), outlined one cell thick, with a pixel-circle dot in the middle (`--pixel` in `scripts/render-icon.swift`). No anti-aliasing on the cells. The smooth alternative draws the menu bar flower in white: an outline 4.5 units wide along the silhouette and a solid dot of radius 5.3 units, the proportions measured from the menu bar template (stroke 3 px and dot 7 px across a 25 px silhouette). The outline follows the silhouette, not the individual petals, so no arcs show where petals overlap. The blur radius is one twelfth of the crop. On the 64-unit canvas, inset the 52 × 52 square by 6 units and use a 12-unit corner radius. `scripts/render-icon.swift` renders `Resources/AppIcon.png` from a photo; `scripts/make-iconset.sh` produces the iconset and ICNS.

## Menu bar icon

Use the pixel-art flower at 21 × 21 pt on the same 21-cell grid as the window glyph, with 1× and 2× PNG representations generated by `scripts/render-icon.swift --pixel-glyph` (1 px and 2 px cells, one-cell outline). The size follows the grid: 21 pt is what puts every cell on whole pixels. Render it as a template image so macOS adjusts its colour for the menu bar background. Its accessibility label is Keylapse. While Keylapse is not working (paused, a permission missing, or the Fn key action still to be set) the centre dot gives way to two pause bars on the same grid: each two cells wide and five tall, one cell apart, filling the dot's 5 × 5 block. The outline does not change, and the label reads Keylapse, not running. The flower in the window and in the menu's Correct Selected Text item always keeps its dot.

## Shortcut keys

Draw flat keys that belong on the glass: white at 18% with a 0.5 pt white edge at 22%, continuous 8 pt corners, no gradients or shadows. Lettering is white and semibold.

Every key is a 38 × 38 pt square like a real keycap. Fn shows `fn` at the top right and a globe at the bottom left; modifier keys show the symbol (`⌃`, `⌥`, `⌘`, `⇧`) at the top right and the lowercase name at 8 pt at the bottom left, with a small L or R at the top left when the side matters; any other key shows its printed name centred (`space`, `F13`, the letter of the current layout). Every shortcut is drawn as one cap per key in Mac order, always joined by `+` in secondary colour. Use white lettering and keep it readable at normal screen size.

The illustrations mirror the current choice and sit at the right edge of the row; the action and its hint fill the rest. The hint names what to press ("Press Control + Fn together", "Press ⌥ Space"); a second line says when a modifier key fires ("Switches when released…") or what macOS does with the same combination ("⌘ Space is used by Spotlight; Keylapse takes it over while it runs"). Clicking the keys records a replacement: modifier keys held together and released on their own, or a combination with a regular key; the border turns accent while recording and the hint says what is expected. A refused recording (a key on its own, Caps Lock, or the same shortcut as the other row) flashes the caps red, turns the hint red with the reason, and keeps recording. Esc, Return or clicking the keys again keeps the current shortcut. A lone key is refused with One key alone won’t do. Hold ⌃, ⌥ or ⌘ with it. The two shortcuts never change each other. Below the group, one secondary line explains how to record, with a Reset button at the right that is invisible while the shortcut is the default so the line never moves.

## Text and feedback

Keep visible text to names, choices and actions. Put timing details and uncommon limitations in help text shown on hover. Make that help available to VoiceOver too.

Errors should name the problem and the next action. An error is a small glass tag, 340 pt wide with 12 pt corners and no title bar: the 17 pt flower at the left, the message in 13 pt medium white. It appears where the chooser would, by the text being fixed, takes no focus and goes away after four seconds. Do not move focus away from the editor for routine feedback. A destination chooser must preserve the editor's selection and allow cancellation through Escape or the native close button.

The destination chooser appears just under the selected text, left edges aligned, 6 pt below it, so the eyes stay where the correction will land; above the text when there is no room below, and always fully on that screen. Apps that do not report where their selection is get it at the pointer, and failing that in the middle of the screen. It fits the longest destination label plus 52 pt for the flower and margins, bounded to 180–360 pt. Use compact 26 pt rows and 12 pt regular labels. The chooser sits on the same frosted glass with only the red close button; its title Correct to is drawn inside the glass to the right of it at 13 pt semibold white, like a window title, with nothing else in the title row: the red button already says how to close, and Escape works without being advertised. The content area is the panel's full size. The 17 pt pixel flower glyph and a white 13% pill mark the selected row and move with keyboard selection; hover brightens the pill to 20%. Keep labels aligned by reserving the glyph column on every row. Avoid system focus rings; use 12 pt semibold labels, white when selected and white at 62% otherwise. Show only the Correct to window title and destination names, without a source caption, footer hints, or Cancel button. Select the first destination immediately. Up/Down and Tab/Shift-Tab move through choices, Return confirms, and Escape, the native close button or a click anywhere outside the chooser cancels; that click still reaches whatever was clicked. Keep the panel non-activating and give it keyboard focus without activating the settings app.

## Checks

Inspect the rendered window, not just the code. Check long layout names, multiple layouts, keyboard focus, permission states and clicks. A design change must not reset preferences, permissions or launch at login.
